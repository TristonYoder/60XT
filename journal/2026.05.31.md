# 60XT Essay
About a year before this challenge, I suffered major back pain. In the spring of 2025, I could not walk without being in excruciating pain. Around that same time, my wife and I found out we were pregnant with our second child. After X-rays and consults, I found a path toward recovery through months of physical therapy, chiropractic care, and stretching. But at 265 lbs, it was also clear I needed to make other changes if I wanted to manage my pain and be the husband and dad I wanted to be.

Through the rest of 2025, I worked my way down to 200 lbs through almost exclusively diet. At the end of the year I met with Jordan a trainer at Life Time, and after a goal-setting session where we discussed my desire to build strength and resilience against back issues, as well as my nervousness about making things worse, he recommended I try the ALPHA class. I started taking one or two a week, and in that initial phase of training the biggest thing I developed was trust that the coaches wouldn’t let me lift with poor form.

This year, our second child was born. Nearing the end of my paternity leave, the combination of stress, dietary drift, and sleeplessness that come with a newborn had taken their toll. I was putting on weight. In a sense, I made my peace with it, physical regression was just part of “this season of life.”

Then a couple of the ALPHA trainers started talking about 60XT. I was telling my wife it would be a great challenge to do sometime, just a bummer that the timing was bad since it was slated to start right as I was returning to work. She challenged me on that, pointing out that for someone who thought it was a bad idea, I sure seemed to be thinking about it a lot, and that if we could figure out how to raise two kids, we could probably figure out a training schedule. So in the first week of April, I returned to work, and started 60XT.

I had three goals. The first was body recomposition dropping fat while retaining as much muscle as possible. The second was finding a recovery rhythm. Serving as the night caretaker so my wife could sleep, I knew intentional recovery would be critical when my sleep was being broken into small chunks by nighttime feedings. Third, I needed a training rhythm that let me make progress while supporting my family well during this transition.

Life Time proved to be central to all three.

For my 60XT consult I met with Jordan again, and we troubleshot an issue in how I was measuring my food. The problem had led me to consistently under-calculate my caloric intake and was part of the reason I had plateaued in my progress over the last couple months. That meeting was the reason my progress did not stay stalled.

The fitness floor gave me everything I needed for my back recovery work on top of my strength training. I could stretch, mobilize, and train without leaving the building. That integration meant I could maximize my time and protect my mobility.

For recovery, I made use of the sauna, cold plunge, and steam room. I had never done a cold plunge before 60XT, but in the spirit of the challenge, I tried it. It’s now just a normal part of my rhythm. My cardiovascular data across 60 days tells the story objectively: my resting heart rate dropped from 65 bpm to 43 bpm at its lowest, and my heart rate variability climbed from 50ms to readings above 100ms. With fragmented sleep as a fixed constraint, contrast therapy was the recovery lever that actually moved the numbers.

The ALPHA programming was the single most physically impactful element of the challenge. It provided a consistent, planned, regimented program I could execute at 6am before my family was awake. Beyond the programming itself, the community made me look forward to class every morning. Tracy, Issy, and Sam pushed me toward endurance and consistent form, and I genuinely value their guidance and corrections as I’ve learned new movements.

Partway through, my three-year-old wanted to start coming to the gym on weekends. One Saturday became one of the most special days my son and I have shared — we went to our respective classes, ate lunch together at the Life Cafe, then spent time at the pool. Life Time stopped being just my gym and became our connection spot.

I started at 205.6 lbs and 18.2% body fat. I ended at 192.8 lbs and 13.5% body fat. I completed Monday through Saturday ALPHA programming with 100% consistency, including a week I was out of town for work. My confidence in my ability to train without injury, and my confidence that I will show up for myself, are higher than they’ve ever been.

The physical changes are real. But what I didn’t expect was the identity shift. I realized part of what happened during this challenge was re-identifying myself as someone who takes care of their body.

Going forward, ALPHA will remain my training foundation. It fits my schedule, it fits my family, and I’ve proven I can sustain it under the hardest possible conditions — a newborn at home, a return to work, travel. If I could do it then, I can do it now.
